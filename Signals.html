What these mean

Plot
What each term in the beta spectrum means
Kinematic variables
W = 1 + T/mec² is the total electron (positron) energy in units of the electron mass, p = √(W² − 1) its momentum in units of mec, W₀ the value at the endpoint, and q = W₀ − W the (anti)neutrino energy. Z is the charge of the daughter nucleus, R its radius (R = √(5/3) times the rms charge radius), α the fine-structure constant and γ = √(1 − (αZ)²).
Phase space, p W (W₀ − W)²
The number of ways to share the decay energy between the electron and the neutrino when the heavy nucleus takes the momentum balance but almost no energy. This alone gives the bell shape of the spectrum.
Fermi function F₀(Z, W)
The Coulomb attraction (β⁻) or repulsion (β⁺) between the outgoing lepton and the daughter nucleus changes the electron wave function at the nucleus. It piles β⁻ spectra up at low energy and pushes β⁺ spectra up in energy. It is the largest correction, a factor of up to several at low energy for heavy nuclei.
Finite nuclear size, L₀ and U
F₀ is computed for a point charge. L₀ corrects it for a uniformly charged sphere of radius R (a few 0.1% to several %), and U for the real, diffuse charge distribution of the nucleus (about 0.1%).
Convolution and induced currents, C
The leptons are created everywhere inside the nucleus, not at a point, so their wave functions must be averaged over the nuclear volume; this gives small terms in W, 1/W and W². For Gamow–Teller decays the same factor carries the induced currents: weak magnetism b/Ac (the weak analogue of the nuclear magnetic moment, fixed by the conserved vector current; about 5 in the impulse approximation; it tilts the spectrum by about ±0.5%/MeV), the induced tensor d/Ac (a "second-class current", zero for mirror decays in the Standard Model), and Λ, the ratio of two nuclear matrix elements (M₁₂₁/M₁₀₁).
Mixing ratio ρ
ρ = CAMGT/(CVMF) sets how much of an allowed decay goes through the Fermi (vector, ΔJ = 0) and the Gamow–Teller (axial, ΔJ = 0 or 1) operators. ρ = 0 is pure Fermi (0⁺→0⁺), ρ = ∞ pure Gamow–Teller. It weights the vector and axial shape factors and recoil terms.
Radiative corrections, R
Virtual photons exchanged between the leptons and the nucleus, and real photons radiated in the decay (inner bremsstrahlung), shift energy around in the spectrum. The order-α part (Sirlin's function g) is universal and a few percent; the O(Zα²) and O(Z²α³) parts depend on the nuclear charge and size. Near the endpoint, soft photons are summed to all orders (Repko–Wu).
Recoil, RN and Q
The nucleus is not infinitely heavy: it recoils, which changes the three-body phase space (RN), and its Coulomb field moves away from the lepton pair (Q). Both are 10⁻⁵ to 10⁻³.
Atomic screening, S
The atomic electrons partly shield the nuclear charge, so the outgoing lepton feels a weaker Coulomb field far from the nucleus. It matters below a few tens of keV (about 1% for a medium-Z β⁻ emitter at 10 keV).
Atomic exchange, X (β⁻ only)
The β electron can be created directly in an empty bound orbital of the daughter while an atomic electron is kicked into the continuum; the two final states cannot be told apart and interfere. This raises the spectrum by up to tens of percent below a few keV (important for ⁶³Ni, ²⁴¹Pu, tritium).
Atomic mismatch, r
The parent's electron cloud does not fit the daughter atom, so the atom is left excited and takes some energy from the leptons. The effect concentrates near the endpoint: on average the spectrum ends ΔE = ½ ∂²B/∂Z² earlier (B: total electron binding energy; ΔE ≈ 30 eV for tritium, 90 eV for ⁶³Ni), which costs about 1% of the spectrum 15 keV below the ⁶³Ni endpoint. The page applies this as the endpoint shift ((W₀ − W − ΔE)/(W₀ − W))², of which Hayen et al.'s r is the first-order expansion.
Forbidden decays and the shape factor
When angular momentum or parity forbid the allowed operators, the leptons must carry orbital angular momentum L = k. For unique k-th forbidden decays (ΔJ = k + 1, parity (−1)ᵏ) only one nuclear matrix element contributes and the shape follows from the lepton momenta: C(W) ∝ Σₙ λₙ p²⁽ⁿ⁻¹⁾ q²⁽ᵏ⁺¹⁻ⁿ⁾ / [(2n−1)! (2(k+1−n)+1)!], where λₙ are Coulomb functions (ratios of generalised Fermi functions, 1 without Coulomb effects). For non-unique decays several nuclear matrix elements interfere; the ξ approximation (allowed shape) is used unless you enter an empirical shape factor 1 + aW + b/W + cW². For forbidden branches the mixing ratio ρ and the allowed weak-magnetism slope have no meaning and are switched off; the finite-size and recoil terms are allowed-decay formulas applied in their axial form (an approximation at the 10⁻³ level). For low-Q unique decays such as ⁹⁰Sr, Seng, Glick-Magid & Cirigliano (PRL 134, 081805, 2025) find that O(α) radiative corrections open the decay at zero nuclear recoil momentum and may distort the spectrum by several per cent; this effect is not included.
β–ν angular correlation a (not in the energy spectrum)
The decay rate also depends on the angle between electron and neutrino, as 1 + a β cos θeν for allowed decays (a = 1 for Fermi, −1/3 for Gamow–Teller transitions in the Standard Model; scalar and tensor couplings change it quadratically, including couplings to right-handed neutrinos that leave bF = 0). Integrating over the neutrino direction removes it from the electron energy spectrum; it is measured through the recoiling nucleus. For unique forbidden decays the correlation has an extra term ∝ [β² − (β·ν̂)²]/q² (Glick-Magid et al. 2017; 2023) that has been proposed as a way to see a, and hence right-handed tensor couplings, in the energy spectrum. When the q² dependence of the nuclear matrix element (|⟨LJ⟩|² ∝ q2(J−1)) is kept, that term cancels the β·ν̂ term exactly on integration, for every J, so to leading order the unique spectrum carries only bF; the same weighting is what produces the standard unique shapes above. The page therefore does not offer a separate a input for spectra.
Fierz term bF (beyond the Standard Model)
Scalar or tensor weak interactions, which do not exist in the Standard Model V−A theory, would interfere with the vector and axial currents and add a term bF γ/W to the spectrum shape. To first order in the new couplings, bF = ±[Re((CS+C′S)/CV) + ρ² Re((CT+C′T)/CA)]/(1+ρ²), with the upper sign for β⁻ (Jackson, Treiman & Wyld 1957). Pure Fermi decays probe scalar and pure Gamow–Teller decays tensor couplings. Unique forbidden decays involve only axial operators, so only tensor couplings interfere, with the same γ/W form: bF = ±Re((CT+C′T)/CA). For non-unique forbidden decays the interference involves several matrix elements, and the page treats bF as a phenomenological tilt. Current limits are |bF| ≲ 10⁻³–10⁻².
Neutrino mass mβ
A massive neutrino needs at least mβc² of the decay energy, so (W₀ − W)² becomes (W₀ − W)√((W₀ − W)² − mβ²) and the spectrum ends mβc² below the endpoint. mβ² = Σ|Uei|² mi² is the effective mass measured by tritium experiments (KATRIN: mβ < 0.45 eV). Visible only in the last few eV of low-endpoint spectra.
Heavy (sterile) neutrino
If the electron neutrino has a small admixture |Ue4|² of a heavy state of mass m₄, a fraction |Ue4|² of decays emit that state: N = (1 − |Ue4|²) N(mβ) + |Ue4|² N(m₄). The heavy branch stops at T₀ − m₄c², producing a kink. Searches for keV-scale sterile neutrinos (a dark-matter candidate) look for this kink at the 10⁻⁶–10⁻⁸ level.

About the data and methods

Decay data: ENSDF via the IAEA LiveChart of Nuclides. Photon cross sections: NIST XCOM (Berger et al., SRD 8). (α,n): the NeuCBOT method and datasets (TALYS-1.95 cross sections and neutron spectra, SRIM stopping powers; Westerdale & Meyers, NIM A 875 (2017) 57). Beta spectra: the corrections reviewed by Hayen et al., Rev. Mod. Phys. 90 (2018) 015008, with unique-forbidden shapes and Coulomb functions after Behrens & Bühring.
