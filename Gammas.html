How candidates are ranked

Each line gets an expected emission rate, in gammas per second per kilogram, summed over the materials you select (equal masses of each) and your detector's own material:

  • Decays: typical activity of the decay-chain segment in each material × branching × line intensity. Activities are medians of the assay records in the radiopurity.org Material Assay Database (J.C. Loach et al., Nucl. Instrum. Meth. A 839 (2016) 6) for that material, or the upper-continental-crust averages of Rudnick & Gao (2003) for rock. Each source's “Sources” list names the data sets used.
  • Neutron capture (n,γ): thermal neutron flux × atoms of the target isotope per kilogram (composition × isotopic abundance) × thermal cross section × gammas per capture.
  • Inelastic scattering (n,n′γ): fast neutron flux × atoms per kilogram × an assumed 1 b gamma-production cross section, shared over the levels. Per-level cross sections are not included yet, so treat these rates as order-of-magnitude.
  • Alpha-induced reactions (α,n), (α,p), (α,α′) …: alpha activity of each chain segment in the material × thick-target gamma yield per alpha, for alphas stopping in that material. Yields are ∫σγ(E)/S(E) dE over each alpha's slowing down, the method of NeuCBOT, using TALYS cross sections and SRIM stopping powers. These matter mainly for light elements (F, Na, Mg, Al, Si, B, O); heavier nuclei are shielded by the Coulomb barrier.
  • Activation products: saturation activity, equal to the capture rate on the parent isotope.
  • Everything else (cosmogenic activation, calibration and medical sources, airborne radon, X-ray fluorescence, annihilation): no rate model yet, so these use a coarse prior on a fixed reference scale.
  • Isotopes with no reason to be there (not a chain member, not cosmogenic, not an activation product, not a common man-made or calibration source) are ranked below every neutron- and alpha-induced line, though still listed.

Default neutron fluxes: underground thermal 1.08×10⁻⁶ n cm⁻² s⁻¹ (P. Belli et al., Il Nuovo Cimento A 101 (1989) 959) and fast ≈ 0.9×10⁻⁷ n cm⁻² s⁻¹ (Gran Sasso, as summarized in R. Bernabei et al., arXiv:1409.3516); surface fast 5.5×10⁻³ n cm⁻² s⁻¹ (integral of the sea-level spectrum of M.S. Gordon et al., IEEE Trans. Nucl. Sci. 51 (2004) 3427), with the surface thermal flux assumed equal to it. Real fluxes vary a lot between sites and inside shielding: enter your own.

Data sources

  • Decay gammas, half-lives, level gammas, isotopic abundances: ENSDF evaluations, retrieved from the IAEA LiveChart of Nuclides. Each source cites its evaluation.
  • Thermal-neutron-capture cascades: level schemes and branching ratios from G4RiversideCASCADE (L. Weimer et al., Nucl. Instrum. Meth. A 1093 (2027) 171940, doi:10.1016/j.nima.2026.171940), built from ENSDF capture evaluations. Line intensities and coincidences are computed here. No level schemes yet for some abundant isotopes, including ¹²⁷I, ¹³³Cs, ¹⁰⁷Ag, ²⁰⁶Pb, ²⁰⁷Pb and ¹⁹⁷Au.
  • Thermal cross sections: H. Rauch and W. Waschkowski, ILL Neutron Data Booklet (2003), via the periodictable package; radiative capture only for ¹⁰B, ⁶Li, ¹⁴N and ³He.
  • Alpha-induced gamma cross sections: TALYS-1.95 (A.J. Koning & D. Rochman, Nucl. Data Sheets 113 (2012) 2841, doi:10.1016/j.nds.2012.11.002), tabulated in the NeuCBOT datasets. Alpha spectra (from ENSDF), chain branchings and stopping-power tables from NeuCBOT (S. Westerdale & P.D. Meyers, Nucl. Instrum. Meth. A 875 (2017) 57, doi:10.1016/j.nima.2017.09.007); stopping powers computed with SRIM (J.F. Ziegler, M.D. Ziegler & J.P. Biersack, Nucl. Instrum. Meth. B 268 (2010) 1818, doi:10.1016/j.nimb.2010.02.091).
  • X-rays: xraylib (T. Schoonjans et al., Spectrochim. Acta B 66 (2011) 776).
  • Material compositions: nominal compositions (listed per material in each source's “Sources”); rock from Rudnick & Gao (2003), doi:10.1016/B0-08-043751-6/03016-4.
  • Context (where sources appear, how to test them): curated by the UCR Dark Matter and Neutrino Lab.

This is a beta. Rankings are guidance, not identifications. Found a wrong number or a missing source? Report it on GitHub.
